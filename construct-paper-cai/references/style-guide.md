# Style Guide

## Style name

**构成主义纸片科幻拼贴 / Constructivist Paper Sci-Fi Collage**

> Use dark paper, geometric cuts, and Constructivist composition to turn science, memory, conflict, and civilization into a tactile retro-futurist archive.

## Mood

Cold, cerebral, mysterious, absurd, historical, and experimental. The work should feel like a recovered scientific dossier, damaged archive, speculative publication, or physical model of an idea.

## Palette

Use these as directional anchors rather than rigid color-matching requirements:

| Role | Color | Approximate hex |
|---|---|---|
| Primary field | Deep navy-black | `#07131C` |
| Structural dark | Charcoal | `#17191B` |
| System accent | Desaturated teal | `#315A60` |
| Secondary cool | Slate blue | `#3D5362` |
| Figure and title | Warm bone-white | `#D6C8AB` |
| Secondary shadow | Dusty purple | `#342C40` |
| Central impact | Rusty vermilion | `#A63F2E` |
| Warning accent | Burnt orange | `#D56B2D` |

Keep roughly 65–75% of the image dark. Allow one dominant rusty-red event. Use orange only as a small signal.

## Material system

- Construction paper and aged archival stock
- Visible fibers, deckled edges, folds, dry pigment, and glue lift
- Low-poly paper facets instead of smooth shading
- Photocopy noise and imperfect screen-print registration
- Small shadows between layers to show physical assembly
- No glossy plastic, glass, airbrush, or pristine vector surfaces

## Figure and subject system

- Remove or reduce facial features.
- Build faces and objects from a small number of angular planes.
- Preserve identity through silhouette and recognizable attributes.
- Use asymmetry and cropped framing.
- Keep figures slightly monumental and sculptural, never cute.
- For non-human subjects, simplify toward one unmistakable silhouette and one visual action.

## Composition system

Use:

- One dominant diagonal axis
- Geometric wedges and stepped scale
- Broken circles, orbital arcs, and technical windows
- Circular or triangular montage crops
- Extreme size contrast
- Purposeful dark negative space
- A clear visual path connecting premise, event, and consequence

Avoid:

- Static bilateral symmetry
- Evenly spaced icon rows
- Filling every corner
- Repeating the same centered portrait across deliverables

## Systems vocabulary

When a subject involves time, knowledge, technology, or civilization, express it as a connected sequence rather than isolated symbols. Example:

`fire → stone tool → wheel → writing/printing → gear/steam → rocket → satellite`

Adapt the sequence to the subject. Keep relationships visible with paths, windows, arrows, orbital rules, scale changes, or overlaps.

## Typography

### Display title

- Construct characters from rectangles, trapezoids, triangles, and circular cuts.
- Use diagonal baselines, staggered blocks, and controlled overlap.
- Layer bone-white and vermilion with black or teal misregistration shadows.
- Treat title blocks as part of the composition architecture.
- Preserve character skeletons and reading order.

### Body, captions, and interface text

- Narrow sans-serif, grotesk, or monospaced technical type
- Cold white on deep navy
- Red for faults and warnings
- Teal for stable states and secondary information
- Short labels and generous spacing

## Series consistency test

Before delivery, confirm:

1. Would the asset still belong to the series with the logo removed?
2. Is the material clearly paper rather than smooth 3D?
3. Is there one dominant red event?
4. Is the composition structural rather than decorative?
5. Are titles and labels fully readable?
6. Does the composition suit its actual format and viewing distance?
